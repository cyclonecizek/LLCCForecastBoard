Sources

    What the numbers mean. Each value is the chance that a rule is violated at some point in the window for the selected pad, estimated from model members. A member counts as a violation if any hour it has inside the window meets the rule's model criterion; sources are weight-averaged and rounded to 5%. The overall value counts a member once if it violates any checked rule, and is never below the lightning row.

    Flight path. A line from the pad along the launch azimuth (see Sources), with distances measured to its nearest point. Lightning uses the hi-res lightning field within 10 nmi and NBM thunderstorm probability. Cumulus uses model reflectivity at the -10 °C level within 5 nmi, or echo tops above the -20 °C height within 10 nmi. Anvil, debris, thick cloud layers and disturbed weather are rough stand-ins built from deep convection, cloud cover by layer and reflectivity. Surface electric fields have no model forecast and are covered in practice by the cumulus and lightning rules; smoke plumes are not forecast; triboelectrification is treated as exempt by vehicle configuration.

    Evidence tiers. Hi-res models (HRRR, HREF members, NAM 3 km) and NBM are direct evidence. Where no direct evidence covers a window (mostly past about 2.5 days), global ensembles supply a stand-in: deep convection is precipitation with CAPE of 1000 J/kg or more. Stand-in cells are hatched and should be read as lower confidence. This is a planning aid, not a substitute for LLCC evaluation by the launch weather officer.
